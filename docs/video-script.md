# Narration script — framework video

The video (`nunez.info/init`, 91 seconds) has no audio. This is the text to record over it,
with the timing of each scene.

The current video predates section 2 of GATE.md, the orchestrator and `/establish`. The scene
"The foundation" below goes between "The thesis" and "The journey" the next time the video is
recorded; until then the video covers the cycle only.

A comfortable pace is about 2.5 words per second. Each block is measured to sit inside its
scene with room to spare, leaving silence at the end.

---

**0:00 – 0:07 · Title**

> GATE. Governed, Assisted, Traceable Engineering. An AI-assisted software development life
> cycle.

**0:07 – 0:21 · The problem**

> Almost every team already uses AI assistants. Almost none has them ordered.
>
> Quality ends up depending on who used the assistant that day, and how well they asked.
>
> Decisions evaporate: a team argues something through, reaches agreement, and three weeks
> later argues the same thing again in another change.
>
> Security arrives once the code is written, which is the most expensive moment.
>
> And nobody can answer which standard was applied to a change, or who approved it.

**0:21 – 0:28 · The thesis**

> Assistance that stops where judgement begins.
>
> GATE adds no capability to the assistant. It orders the ones it already has.

**New scene, about 0:10 · The foundation**

> GATE is not the SDLC a company runs. It is what a company builds its own SDLC from.
>
> An orchestrator chooses the AI assistant, answers an interview, and GATE builds the SDLC with
> them, layer by layer, until a verification comes out green.

**0:28 – 0:42 · The journey**

> The cycle has thirteen stages across four legs.
>
> Product defines the problem and sets criteria that can be checked.
>
> Engineering refines, settles the architecture, builds, and goes through two distinct reviews
> before functional verification.
>
> Operations deploys, keeps the service up, and goes back to ask whether the original problem
> was solved.
>
> Everything learned ends up in a standards catalog.
>
> Each stage has its input, its output and its owner.

**0:42 – 0:56 · Five moments**

> Thirteen stages sounds like a lot. But the number that matters is another one.
>
> A person invokes at five points: when the need is born, when someone sits down to build,
> when someone reviews, when someone tests, and when someone deploys.
>
> The framework chains everything else.
>
> And that matters, because mechanisms that depend on someone remembering to invoke them
> simply do not happen.

**0:56 – 1:10 · The line**

> The whole framework rests on a split that admits no exceptions.
>
> The AI produces and verifies: it reads, correlates, drafts every artefact, and checks against
> the source rather than against its memory.
>
> The person judges and authorises: accepts or rejects at every control point, chooses when
> something has to be given up, authorises every write into a shared system, and answers for
> the outcome.
>
> When one of those four moves to the other side, the framework stops applying, however
> unchanged everything else looks.

**1:10 – 1:22 · The loop closes**

> What makes this improve with use is the last stage.
>
> A decision taken in a review becomes a rule, and that rule applies before the next line of
> code is written.
>
> The same goes for the root cause of an incident. Those usually yield better rules, because
> nobody argues with an outage that already happened.

**1:22 – 1:31 · Close**

> GATE is in use and under continuous improvement. The full document, the skill contracts and
> the templates are published and free to use.

## Recording notes

Record somewhere without echo and leave half a second of silence between blocks: it is easier
to align afterwards than to trim.

If a block runs long, cut its last sentence rather than speeding up. The scene can carry
silence; it cannot carry a rush.

To regenerate the silent video, the animation is in the project history and records with
Playwright at 1280 by 720.
